# Codec 2 3200: format, decoder and encoder (clean-room spec)

**Status:** revision 5, 2026-11-25. Format, decoder, and the encoder's quantizers. The encoder's analysis
methods are the published ones, and the library may use its own (section 10.2), held to section 10.3.
**Written by:** the spec writer, from `codec2.pdf` (read as a document, nothing copied), black-box runs of upstream's
`c2enc`, `c2dec` and `c2sim`, the codebook data files read as lists of numbers, and the answers of a separate
source reader who read three upstream files under the owner's approval (`defines.h`, `quantise.c`, `codec2.c`, upstream
commit `310777b`). The spec writer received only those answers, as numbers or one sentence, and tags them
`[src: <file> @ 310777b]`; no upstream source was opened by the spec writer. This revision depends on the three
2026-11-25 source-reader rows of the reading log in `docs/clean-room.md`.
**Read this first:** [How complete this is](#how-complete-this-is). The frame format and the quantizers, in both directions, are settled.
The envelope, interpolation and levels are settled to within about 1 dB. The phase model, the coded synthesis window, the exact post-filter exponents (a range is
given) and what turns the voicing bits into voiced sub-frames in mixed frames are **not** settled and are listed as
open questions ([Open questions](#open-questions)); an implementer builds those parts from the description here and
tunes them against `c2dec` output. The encoder's pitch search and voicing post-rules are published only in part
(section 10.2); the library may differ from them inside section 10.3.

## 1. Terms

| Term here | Codec 2 docs | RadioFoundry | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| codec frame | frame | (audio) block | 20 ms, 160 samples, 64 bits at 3200 |
| sub-frame | 10 ms frame | — | Codec 2 analyses and synthesizes every 10 ms (80 samples); a 3200 codec frame describes two. Here: sub-frame **a** (first) and **b** (second) |
| stream frame | — | — | M17's 40 ms frame: two codec frames. M17 code only, never in the library |
| `w0` | Wo | `mbe_params_t.w0` | Fundamental. Stored as in `core/mbe/mbe.h`: phase step per sample, 2^32 = one turn |

| voicing | voicing | `v[]` | One bit per sub-frame at 3200 |

Also: `L` is the number of harmonics, `L = floor(pi / w0)` `[pdf §3.2]`; `m` is the harmonic number 1..L; `Fs` = 8000 Hz;
frequencies in Hz unless a symbol says radians per sample.

## 2. The 64-bit frame

### 2.1 Layout

One codec frame is **8 bytes = 64 bits**. Bit 0 is the first transmitted bit and is the **most significant bit of
byte 0**; bit 63 is the least significant bit of byte 7. Fields are packed in this order with no padding
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x01_header_and_layout.py]`, `[exp: tools/blackbox/x02_pitch_field.py]`,
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x03_energy_field.py]`, `[exp: tools/blackbox/x04_lsp_fields.py]`:

| Bits | Width | Field | Coding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | voicing of sub-frame a (the first, interpolated one) `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]` | 1 = voiced. See 3.1 and OQ-1 |
| 1 | 1 | voicing of sub-frame b (the second one, carrying `w0`, energy and LSPs) `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]` | 1 = voiced. See 3.1 and OQ-1 |
| 2-8 | 7 | `w0` index `i`, 0..127 | Gray coded, MSB first |
| 9-13 | 5 | energy index `e`, 0..31 | Gray coded, MSB first |
| 14-18 | 5 | LSP delta index `d1` (table `dlsp1`) | Gray coded |
| 19-23 | 5 | `d2` (`dlsp2`) | Gray coded |
| 24-28 | 5 | `d3` (`dlsp3`) | Gray coded |
| 29-33 | 5 | `d4` (`dlsp4`) | Gray coded |
| 34-38 | 5 | `d5` (`dlsp5`) | Gray coded |
| 39-43 | 5 | `d6` (`dlsp6`) | Gray coded |
| 44-48 | 5 | `d7` (`dlsp7`) | Gray coded |
| 49-53 | 5 | `d8` (`dlsp8`) | Gray coded |
| 54-58 | 5 | `d9` (`dlsp9`) | Gray coded |
| 59-63 | 5 | `d10` (`dlsp10`) | Gray coded |

Total 2 + 7 + 5 + 50 = 64. This matches the allocation in the PDF `[pdf §2.5, table 1]` (pitch 7, spectral
amplitudes 50, energy 5, voicing 2). The order and the Gray coding are not in the PDF; they are from experiment.

**How the layout was found.** Flipping each of the 64 bits of a voiced frame and decoding showed: bits 0-8 change the
pitch of the output (bits 0-1 turn the voicing off, giving noise), bits 9-13 change only the level, bits 14-63
change only the spectral shape (x01). Encoding tones and pulse trains of known pitch showed that the first nine bits
carry a nearly monotone number only after Gray decoding bits 2-8, and the bits above bit 8 do not depend on pitch
(x02). A frame is unambiguous: a worked example is `d6 75 ef 7b de f7 bd ef` hex, voicing 1,1, `i` = 55, `e` = 22 and
all ten LSP indices 10; it decodes to f0 = 200.39 Hz.

### 2.2 Gray code

Every multi-bit field is the **binary-reflected Gray code** of its index `[exp: x02, x03, x04]`:
`gray = n XOR (n >> 1)`; the inverse is `n = gray XOR (gray >> 1) XOR (gray >> 2) XOR ...` until the shifted term is 0.
Indices are decoded with the inverse before use; the tables below are indexed by the decoded index. Evidence: with
the raw field values as the sweep variable the decoded pitch, level and spectral centroid are not monotone, with the
Gray-decoded value they are (x02 table, x03 table, x04 part A).

### 2.3 File header (not part of the frame)

`c2enc` writes a 7-byte header **only when the output file name ends in `.c2`**: `c0 de c2 01 02 <mode> 00`, with
the mode byte 0x00 for 3200 (0x01 for 2400, 0x02 for 1600, 0x08 for 700C). `c2dec` accepts a file with or without it
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x01_header_and_layout.py]`. M17 carries bare 8-byte frames, and the library takes bare frames.
The frames are identical with and without the header. (This resolves the 7-byte offset seen on first use.)

## 3. Quantizers and dequantizers

### 3.1 Voicing

Two bits, one per sub-frame, 1 = voiced. The first bit read is the voicing of sub-frame a and the second that of
sub-frame b, and each sub-frame's voiced flag is set directly from its own bit; nothing else in those three files
changes it `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]`.

What the black-box runs show does not look like that, and the difference is not yet explained
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x08_voicing_and_state.py]`: in steady state only (1,1) decodes periodic, (1,0) decodes bit for bit
identical to (0,0), and (0,1) decodes unvoiced-like. So something after the decoder step that sets the flags (the pitch
interpolation, the phase synthesis and the synthesis itself live in files that were not read) must demote or ignore
sub-frame a's voiced flag when sub-frame b is unvoiced, or when the previous sub-frame was unvoiced. What is known
about the pitch of a voiced sub-frame a in mixed frames is in section 4 (x10). The rest stays OQ-1.

### 3.2 `w0` (7 bits)

Dequantize with `f0 = 50 + 350 * i / 128` Hz, `i` the decoded index 0..127 `[exp: x02]`. In radians per sample:

```
w0 = 2*pi * f0 / 8000 = Wmin + i * (Wmax - Wmin) / 128,   Wmin = 2*pi/160,  Wmax = 2*pi/20
```

So index 0 is 50.000 Hz (period 160 samples), index 127 is 397.27 Hz, and 400 Hz would be index 128 and is never
produced. Measured f0 matched this to 0.001 Hz at 14 indices (largest error 0.5 Hz, at the two highest indices,
where harmonic 10 is faint and the peak search is unreliable; the 12-13 Hz in the x02 deviation column there is that, not a real offset: with a flat envelope harmonic 10 sits exactly at `m * f0`) and the harmonics of the decoded signal sit exactly at `m * f0`. The
alternative divisor 127 (so that index 127 = 400 Hz) was measured and rejected: at index 127 the decoder gave
396.8 to 397.3 Hz, not 400. For the library's phase step: `w0_u32 = round(2^32 * f0 / 8000)`.

The range matches the stated pitch estimation range, 50-400 Hz `[pdf §3.4]`. The first harmonic is scaled down at low
pitch, see 6.4. Every one of the 128 indices decodes to a periodic voiced signal (x08 part E).

### 3.3 Energy (5 bits)

Decoded index `e` = 0..31. The output level rises by **1.5625 dB per step** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x03_energy_field.py]`
(measured 1.56-1.57 dB per step over e = 3..23, least-squares slope 1.5656; 50/32 = 1.5625; the steps near e = 0 are
noisier because the output is only a few counts). Values near
e = 31 saturate at the 16-bit output range. How `e` sets the absolute level, together with the envelope, is in 6.3.

What the encoder measures, and how it chooses the index, is in section 10. The PDF describes the energy as the mean level of the harmonic
amplitudes, and elsewhere as the LPC energy `E = G^2` `[pdf §2.5, §3.7]`.

### 3.4 LSPs (50 bits)

Ten scalar quantizers on **differences**. For `j` = 1..10, the decoded index `d_j` selects a value `D_j[d_j]` in Hz from
table `j`; the dequantized LSP frequencies are the running sum `[exp: tools/blackbox/x04_lsp_fields.py]`:

```
f_1 = D_1[d_1]        f_j = f_(j-1) + D_j[d_j]     (Hz),   w_j = 2*pi * f_j / 8000   (radians)
```

This is "LSP differences are quantised" `[pdf §4]` made exact: the first difference is relative to 0 Hz. Tables
(values in Hz, 32 entries each, index 0 first):

| Table | Values |
|---|---|
| `dlsp1`, `dlsp2`, `dlsp3`, `dlsp7`, `dlsp8`, `dlsp9`, `dlsp10` | 25, 50, 75, ... , 800 (step 25: entry `k` is `25*(k+1)`) |
| `dlsp4`, `dlsp5`, `dlsp6` | 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 175, 200, then 250, 300, ... , 1400 (step 50: entry `k >= 8` is `200 + 50*(k-7)`) |

(from the codebook files `dlsp1.txt` .. `dlsp10.txt` in upstream's `src/codebook/`, each a header line `1 32`
then 32 numbers; these are the only codebook files the 3200 decoder uses; see "What the PDF gives" below.) The tables are
generated by the two rules above, so they can be produced by formula, but the implementation's table importer should
still check them value by value against the files.

Group order: a sweep of each group with the others held (x04 part A) shows group 1 (bits 14-18) moves the whole
spectrum the most and group 10 (bits 59-63) the least, as a chain that starts at 0 Hz must. The decoded spectrum
follows the model built from this running sum with a regression slope of 1.03 (x04 part B).

The unit conversion between table Hz and radians is `w = 2*pi*f/8000` `[exp: x04]` (equivalently f / 4000 * pi).
The library can hold LSPs as integers in Hz; every table value is a multiple of 25 Hz,
every sum is too.

**Sums above 4 kHz and other out-of-range cases.** The maximum sum is 9800 Hz. Such frames decode without error and the
output stays bounded (x08 part D), but what the decoder does to the LSPs (clamping, forced ordering or minimum
separation) is answered by the source read: the 3200 decoder builds the LSPs as the running sum above (table Hz to
radians by pi/4000), interpolates them, and passes them straight to the LSP to LPC step with **no clamp, reorder or
minimum separation**; those checks exist only in the decoders of other modes `[src: quantise.c, codec2.c @ 310777b]`.
So a bit-compatible decoder must not add any. c2fx may still add its own guard against unstable filters as a
`[c2fx design]` choice (the effect on frames from `c2enc`, which are always valid, is nil); this spec does not specify
one. Frames from `c2enc` never exercise this (its LSP set is derived from a valid LPC).

## 4. Sub-frames and interpolation

A codec frame decodes to two sub-frames, **a** (first 10 ms) and **b** (second 10 ms). The fields describe **b**.
Sub-frame a is derived by interpolating between the previous frame's b parameters and this frame's `[pdf §2.4, §3.7]`
(the PDF says linear interpolation at the 10 ms rate; the weights are from the experiments below).

**Timing** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x07_interpolation.py]`: a frame `k` covers output samples `160k .. 160k+159`. Its
sub-frame a is centered on sample `160k + 80` and its sub-frame b on `160k + 160` (the first sample of the next frame).
Amplitudes follow straight ramps between those centers: the envelope of a harmonic was a piecewise-linear ramp whose
knots are the sub-frame values at those times. This is what a triangular overlap-add window of length 160 and hop 80
produces `[pdf §3.3]`.

| Parameter | Sub-frame a value | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Midpoint in **dB** of previous-b and current-b (the geometric mean of the amplitudes) | x07 part A: at knot a the amplitude was 243.9 vs geometric mean 240.7 (arithmetic mean 344.9); 293.4 vs 288.0 (642.1); falling and rising steps agree |
| LSPs | Midpoint of the two sets, in Hz (weight 0.5) | x07 part B: best weight 0.5 in 3 of 4 cases (0.6 in the other) with rms error at the best weight of 0.6-1.4 dB against 2-8 dB at weights 0 and 1 |
| `w0` | Midpoint of the two `w0` values (weight 0.5, in Hz or radians alike) when the previous frame's sub-frame b was voiced and sub-frame a is voiced (tested with both bits of the current frame set); the **current frame's** `w0` unchanged when the previous frame's sub-frame b was unvoiced (including the very first frame) | `[pdf §2.4]` describes the average; `[exp: tools/blackbox/x10_w0_interpolation.py]`: a harmonic-comb search at the center of sub-frame a found 187.0, 215.0, 257.5 Hz against midpoints 186.7, 214.1, 255.1 Hz for voiced-to-voiced steps, and the current pitch (242.0, 269.0, 188.5 Hz) after unvoiced frames. The routine itself is in a file that was not read (it is called with the previous model, the new one and the lowest `w0`) `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]`. One case (unvoiced then voiced at a lower pitch) gave a comb result at a multiple of the pitch and is not counted |
| Voicing | Set from its own bit `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]`; the black-box behaviour in mixed frames differs | section 3.1, OQ-1 |

Sub-frame b uses the current frame's decoded values directly. The decoder keeps the b values (w0, voicing, energy
index in dB, LSPs in Hz) for the next frame.

**Start-up.** The decoder's initial previous-b state is `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]`: previous voiced flag 0; previous
harmonic amplitudes 1..160 all 0; previous LSP `i` (i = 0..9) equal to `i * pi / 11` radians (so LSP 0 is 0); previous
energy 1 (the source read gives the value only; its unit is not stated); previous `w0` = `2*pi` divided by the longest
pitch period the decoder accepts, and previous `L` = `pi` over that `w0`, truncated. The value of that longest period
is not in the files read, so the initial `w0` and `L` are unknown. They cannot be observed from output either: with the
previous flag 0, a voiced first frame takes the current `w0` in sub-frame a (x10 part B), so the initial `w0` never
reaches the output.

Observed: a single frame decodes to 160 samples, and with identical frames the output level ramps from near zero to
full over the first frame (x08 part C: block rms 59, 171, 268, 458, then steady 607).

`[c2fx design]` defaults, since the unit of the initial energy is unknown and the initial phase is not known: the
previous energy starts at the lowest level the energy quantizer can express (index 0), the other previous values are as
listed, the initial phases are zero, and the previous `w0` is any value (it never reaches the output).

## 5. LSP to LPC

The usual construction, with the tenth-order polynomials of `[pdf §3.7, eq. 33-34]`, confirmed against the decoder
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x04_lsp_fields.py]`:

```
P(z) = (1 + z^-1)  * Product_{i=1,3,5,7,9}  (1 - 2*cos(w_i) * z^-1 + z^-2)
Q(z) = (1 - z^-1)  * Product_{i=2,4,6,8,10} (1 - 2*cos(w_i) * z^-1 + z^-2)
A(z) = (P(z) + Q(z)) / 2          a_0 = 1, coefficients a_1..a_10 of z^-1 .. z^-10
```

(the PDF's printed formula has a misplaced bracket; this is the usual form). `H(z) = G / A(z)`. The decoder's spectrum
follows `1/|A|` computed this way: the regression of measured harmonic level on `-20 log10 |A(f)|` over 40 random
frames had slope 1.03 and residual 1.0 dB once the post-filter terms below are included (x04 part B), so the
frequencies, ordering and bandwidths are right.

## 6. The envelope at the harmonics

For each sub-frame the decoder needs `A_m`, the amplitude of harmonic `m` = 1..L.

### 6.1 The form

`[pdf §3.7, eq. 35-36]`: take the DFT of the LPC model `H(k)`, apply the post filter to its power spectrum, then set
`A_m^2` to the **sum** of the (post-filtered, energy-scaled) power over the DFT bins of harmonic `m`'s band.

The DFT is a real FFT of the 11 LPC coefficients of size `FFT_ENC` = **512**, of which the 256 power bins 0..255 are
used, 2*pi/512 radians per bin `[src: defines.h, quantise.c @ 310777b]`. With `r = 2*pi/512` rad per bin, the band of
harmonic `m` is `lo <= k < hi` with `lo = trunc((m - 0.5) * w0 / r + 0.5)` and
`hi = min(trunc((m + 0.5) * w0 / r + 0.5), 256)`, so the upper bin is excluded and bins above 255 never enter
`[src: quantise.c @ 310777b]` for the edges and the cap. The source read gives the cap as "256" without saying whether
the upper edge is included; the half-open reading is the one the experiment supports: with it the fit of x09 reaches 0.74 dB rms at the
best pair, with the upper bin included it is 1.07 dB (gamma 0.6, beta 0.275; 0.99 against 1.04 dB for the PDF pair). This is
the PDF's band `a_m .. b_m` with truncation in place of rounding and a cap. Measured: the total harmonic power of the
output divided by the sum of band sums of `1/|A|^2` over the harmonic bands, with `N_dft` = 512, is constant (28.2-29.3 dB,
standard deviation 0.8-1.1 dB per frame) from f0 = 64 Hz to 397 Hz (x03 part B), whereas the same quantity taken from
point samples of `1/|A|^2` at the harmonic bins drifts from 34.5 to 43.7 dB over that range (x03 prints both columns). So the amplitude is a band
**sum** (it grows with the number of bins in the band), not a point sample or a band mean.

`N_dft`: the PDF gives 512 only for the pitch estimator's DFT. The source read gives `FFT_ENC` = 512 for this envelope
DFT and `FFT_DEC` = 512 for the synthesis inverse FFT `[src: defines.h, codec2.c @ 310777b]`, so **both are 512**. The
experiments could not tell 256 from 2048 (x05 part C), which is why they never settled it.

### 6.2 Post filter and bass boost

`c2dec --nopf` has **no effect** at 3200: the output is identical bit for bit with and without it
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x05_post_filter.py]`. The post filter and the low-frequency boost are on at creation and the 3200
decoder passes both through unchanged, so they are always applied `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]`.

**Chain** `[src: quantise.c @ 310777b]`, except the floor `eps`, which is `[exp: tools/blackbox/x19_lsp_spacing.py]`; per sub-frame, on the 256 power bins `P(k)` of `1/(|A|^2 + eps)`:

1. Post filter: `P'(k) = P(k) * |A(z/gamma) / A(z)|^(2*beta)` at the bin frequency (`z` on the unit circle), as in the
   PDF's formula below. The source passes `beta` and `gamma` as arguments with defaults from two constants whose values
   are in a file that was not read.
2. Rescale so the sum of `P'` over the 256 bins equals the sum of `P` (each sum having `1E-4` added before the ratio).
3. Multiply by the decoded energy as a linear power, `10^(dB/10)` (6.3).
4. Bass boost: multiply the power in bins 0 to 63 (about 0 to 1 kHz) by 1.4 * 1.4 = **1.96** (+2.92 dB; the source's own
   comment says 3 dB). This is after the rescale and the energy scaling, and exists only inside the post filter.
5. Each harmonic amplitude is the square root of the sum of the bins of its band (6.1); then 6.4's first-harmonic factor.

**Floor on the power response** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x19_lsp_spacing.py]`: no source names it, and the value is experimental. A small
constant `eps` is added to `|A(k)|^2` (the squared magnitude of the 512-point transform of the LPC polynomial, `a_0` = 1) before
it is inverted, so `P(k) = 1 / (|A(k)|^2 + eps)`. It matters only where `|A|^2` is close to `eps`, that is at the peaks of an
envelope whose LSPs are packed tightly.

- **The gap** (x19 part A; w0 index 100, energy index 4, LSPs 175, 175+s, 175+2s Hz). The float decoder without a floor is
  louder than `c2dec` by 11.32, 3.15, 0.98, 0.55 dB at s = 25, 50, 75, 100 Hz and by 0.35 to 0.47 dB from 125 Hz up. The
  smallest `|A(k)|^2` over the 256 bins is 2.4e-8, 6.7e-7, 5.9e-6, 2.8e-5 for s = 25, 50, 75, 100 Hz. At 25 Hz the strongest
  harmonic is 84.6 dB in the float model and 73.3 dB in `c2dec`.
- **Other rules do not fit** (x19 part B, rms error in dB over 32 tight frames, speech-like frames in brackets; none 3.81
  (0.48)): bandwidth expansion of the LPC by 0.99 / 0.985 / 0.98 gives 2.09 / 1.80 / 1.91 (0.44 / 0.57 / 0.72); minimum LSP
  spacing 40 / 50 / 60 Hz gives 2.78 / 2.78 / 2.99; a cap on the spectrum peak of 10 or 30 times the mean gives 1.94 / 2.97.
- **The floor fits**: tight-frame rms 2.03 (2e-7), 1.42 (4e-7), 1.03 (6e-7), 0.77 (8e-7), **0.61 (1e-6)**, 0.53 (1.25e-6), 0.58
  (1.5e-6), 0.84 (2e-6), 1.39 (3e-6), 2.24 (5e-6), 3.58 (1e-5), with speech at 0.48 for every value.
- **At other cluster positions** (x19 part B2, one frame each, rms in dB without a floor, with 1e-6, with 1.5e-6): LSPs 2 to 4
  near 200 Hz 11.22, 0.53, 0.89; LSPs 5 to 7 near 1.1 kHz 4.71, 1.37, 0.91; LSPs 8 to 10 near 2.1 kHz 1.73, 0.90, 0.71.
- **Value of `eps`.** The tight frames alone prefer 1.25e-6 (0.53 dB) and cannot separate 1e-6 from 1.5e-6 (0.61, 0.58). The file
  level does (x19 part C): at 1e-6 the remaining model-minus-`c2dec` gap is +0.32 dB on `sine_200hz` and -0.33 dB on

  file-level residual (and a round number in the range).
- **Inside the post-filter ratio** (x19 part B, tight rms, speech 0.48 throughout): flooring both `|A(z/gamma)|^2` and

  the ratio of step 1; leave `|A(z/gamma)|^2` in the numerator as it is. The step-2 rescale uses the floored `P` and `P'`.

Description `[pdf §3.7, eq. 37]`: `P_f(w) = g * R_w(w)^beta`, `R_w = A(z/gamma) / A(z)` (bandwidth-expanded numerator,
coefficients `a_k * gamma^k`), with `beta = 0.2`, `gamma = 0.5` given in the PDF, `g` chosen so the filter keeps the overall

**Regression** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x04_lsp_fields.py part B]`: harmonic level (dB, 300-3400 Hz) on `-20 log10|A|`,

### 6.3 Level

**Dequantizer** `[src: quantise.c @ 310777b]`: the energy in dB is `E_MIN_DB + e * (E_MAX_DB - E_MIN_DB) / 2^5`, with no
half-step offset, so the top level (e = 31) is one step below `E_MAX_DB`; the power is `10^(dB/10)` and scales the
(post-filtered) LPC power spectrum as in 6.2 step 3. The values of `E_MIN_DB`, `E_MAX_DB` were not in the files read.
From the measured 1.5625 dB per step, `E_MAX_DB - E_MIN_DB` = 32 * 1.5625 = **50 dB** (x03; the least-squares slope 1.5656
gives 50.1, so 50 within 0.2 dB). The values themselves follow from the quantized energy column of the repository's
own `vectors/*_params.csv` (c2sim's record after the 3200 quantizers, from the same upstream commit), below:
**`E_MIN_DB` = -10 dB**, and with the dequantizer form above (and the measured step, so a range of 50 dB) **`E_MAX_DB` = 40 dB** `[src: quantise.c @ 310777b]` for the form. This takes c2sim's energy after the 3200 quantizers to be the value the decoder's dequantizer produces; the form of the dequantizer is the same expression, and the vectors' energies are on its grid.

**Energy scale** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x18_energy_crosslink.py part A]`: all 4520 sub-frame energies in the 14 vector files equal
`10^((-10 + e * 50/32) / 10)` for an integer `e`, the worst relative error being 5.3e-8 (float32 rounding), and all 32
indices occur. Index 0 is 0.1 (-10 dB) and index 31 is 38.44 dB; the vectors never reach 40 dB, which is one step above index 31. This is the same scale as
the encoder's (10.1.3).

**Offset** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x09_post_filter_fit.py]`: with the chain of 6.2 and unit energy power, the measured harmonic
power (dB, 20 log10 of the sinusoid peak amplitude) at e = 20 is higher than the chain's band-sum power by the "offset at
e=20" printed for the chosen pair: **27.63 dB for gamma 0.6, beta 0.275** (so `E_MIN_DB` plus the synthesis gain, both in dB, is
27.63 - 20 * 1.5625 = **-3.62 dB**), or 27.16 dB for the PDF pair (-4.09 dB). With `E_MIN_DB` = -10 dB the synthesis gain
(the IDFT scaling) is therefore **+6.38 dB** for gamma 0.6, beta 0.275 and **+5.91 dB** for the PDF pair. The
per-harmonic level then follows from the chain with 0.74 dB rms residual (0.99 dB for the PDF pair) (x09), counting
harmonics within 40 dB of the frame's strongest.

**Synthesis gain** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x18_energy_crosslink.py]`: 27.63 - 21.25 = **6.38 dB** for the refined pair and
27.16 - 21.25 = **5.91 dB** for the PDF pair (21.25 dB is `E_MIN_DB` plus 20 steps). Both are about 6.0 dB, a factor 2 in
amplitude, which is what a real sinusoid built from a one-sided spectrum has against its complex amplitude (20 log10 2 =
6.02 dB). With the offset fixed at that value (27.27 dB) and no free constant, x18 refits to 0.81 dB rms (gamma 0.35, beta
0.2; 0.82 dB at gamma 0.6, beta 0.275; 0.99 dB for the PDF pair), against 0.74 dB with a free constant. So the gain is
the pair's offset minus 21.25 dB, about 6 dB, and a factor 2 is consistent with both pairs (6.38 - 6.02 = 0.36 dB and 6.02 - 5.91 = 0.11 dB). The
recommended defaults above are unchanged.

The level applies to each sub-frame's own (interpolated, for sub-frame a) energy and LSPs. The PDF's `E = G^2` reading
(the LPC gain squared) fits: the energy sets an overall scale and the shape comes from `1/|A|` with `a_0 = 1`.

**Overload** `[exp: tools/blackbox/x19_lsp_spacing.py part D]`: on two frames, when the signal would reach a high level `c2dec` does not
saturate; its output falls as the energy index rises. Frame 40 of `sine_200hz_clipped` (w0 index 94, LSP indices 7 2 13 3 18 6 21
15 14 20): the level of the first harmonic relative to the chain is -0.5 dB at energy indices 24 to 27 (the largest sample the signal
would have without a limit is 28072 at index 27) and then -1.2, -4.4, -7.5, -10.6 dB at indices 28 to 31 (33604, 40227, 48155, 57646);
the largest `c2dec` samples are 24380, 29185, 32562, 27201, 22723, 18981 at indices 26 to 31 and the float model holds 32767 from
index 28. A tight frame (LSPs 175, 200, 225 Hz, w0 index 100, indices 10 to 18): -0.5 dB through index 14 (unlimited peak 25851),
then -2.4, -5.6, -8.7, -11.8 dB at indices 15 to 18 (30946, 37045, 44346, 53086). On both frames, once it starts, the output falls by
1.5625 dB per energy step (the relative level drops by 3.1 dB per step because the chain rises by the same amount). What does
**not** generalize: the level at which it starts and the size of the first step. The first falling step is -0.7 dB at an unlimited
peak of 33604 on one frame and -1.9 dB at 30946 on the other, so no single threshold is established. Control: frames with wide
spacing (LSP indices all 10, w0 indices 60 and 100) show no fall from index 20 to 31 (`c2dec` rms 939 to 6798 and 907 to 6560;
float model over `c2dec` +0.36 to +0.37 dB throughout), so it follows the level and not the index. It is not an int16 wrap of the
unlimited signal (wrapped rms 23188, 22216, 20019 at indices 29 to 31 on the first frame against the measured 17304, 14455,
12075). The rule that makes it fall was not found (OQ-13).

### 6.4 Which harmonics exist

- `L = floor(pi / w0)` `[pdf §3.2]`. Harmonics are exactly at `m * f0` (x02).
- **Harmonic 1 is multiplied by 0.032 in amplitude (about -30 dB) when `w0 < pi * 150 / 4000` rad**
  (0.1178 rad, a 150 Hz fundamental), applied to each sub-frame right after the envelope step, so for sub-frame a with
  its interpolated `w0` `[src: quantise.c, codec2.c @ 310777b]`. The experiment saw harmonic 1 28 to 64 dB down for
  decoded `i` <= 36 (f0 <= 148.4 Hz) and at full level for `i` >= 37 (f0 >= 151.2 Hz), harmonics 2 and up unaffected
  `[exp: tools/blackbox/x06_first_harmonic.py]`; the -30 dB readings (e = 22) are the factor, the deeper ones are
  low-level measurement floor. The threshold 150 Hz is exact, so 150 Hz itself
  (`w0` = 0.1178097) is not scaled; the grid index 37 is 151.2 Hz.
- Harmonics predicted more than 40 dB below the strongest in their sub-frame are not reproduced by a steady-tone
  measurement (they decode with random phase, section 7), so a comparison with `c2dec` should leave them out.

## 7. Phase model (voiced and unvoiced)

State: **open**; described from the PDF `[pdf §3.6]`, which says only what follows. Behaviours that were measured
are marked.

1. Each harmonic's phase is the sum of an **excitation phase** `phi_m` and the phase of the synthesis filter at that
   harmonic: `theta_m = phi_m + arg H(e^{j m w0})`.
2. **Voiced**: the excitation is a pulse train. The first harmonic's excitation phase advances by `N * w0` per
   sub-frame (`N` = 80 samples): `phi_1[l] = phi_1[l-1] + N*w0`, and `phi_m = m * phi_1` for `m` = 2..L. Measured
   consistent with this: in a steady voiced decode the harmonics sit exactly at `m * f0` (x02), which is what a phase
   that advances by `m * w0 * N` per sub-frame gives.
3. **Unvoiced**: the excitation phase of each harmonic is drawn uniformly from (-pi, pi) every sub-frame, and the PDF
   says to use `F0 = 50 Hz` (`L` = 80) so as to approximate noise. The decoder is **deterministic** (the same input
   always gives the same samples, x08 part B), so its noise generator has a fixed seed. For an unvoiced sub-frame
   the output was **not** independent of the transmitted `w0` (x08 part B: w0 index 5 and 100 gave different
   samples), so how `w0` and `L` are used for unvoiced frames is OQ-1/OQ-5.
4. The filter phase `arg H` is the **minimum-phase** response matching the magnitude response of `1/A` sampled at the
   harmonic centers, bin `round(m * w0 / r)`, with `r` = 2*pi/512 radians per bin `[pdf §3.6, eq. 36]`; the standard way is the cepstrum (or the Hilbert relation
   between log magnitude and phase) (textbook material, unverified). Which
   construction and which sizes: OQ-5.
5. A noise-floor rule: the decoder tracks the background level seen during unvoiced frames, and a harmonic under
   that level gets random phase, i.e. is treated as unvoiced `[pdf §3.6]`. Constants: OQ-5.
6. No attempt is made to keep the final phase (after the filter) continuous across sub-frames; only the excitation
   phase is continuous `[pdf §3.6, note 5]`.

Because randomness only perturbs phases, **bit-exact random numbers are not needed** for a log-spectral-distance
comparison with `c2dec`; any uniform generator seeded per decoder instance is acceptable, so long as it is deterministic
`[c2fx design]`.

## 8. Synthesis

`[pdf §3.3]`, with the timing confirmed by the amplitude ramps measured in x07:

1. For each sub-frame fill a spectrum array of `N_dft` bins with zeros; for each harmonic `m` = 1..L put the complex
   value `A_m * exp(j*theta_m)` at bin `round(m * w0 / r)` (`r` = 2*pi/512 radians per bin, as in 6.1), and its conjugate at the mirrored bin so the
   result is real `[pdf eq. 10-11]`.
2. Inverse DFT: real output, `N_dft` = `FFT_DEC` = **512** `[src: defines.h @ 310777b]` (> 2N = 160 `[pdf §3.3]`).
   The hop is `N_S` = 10 ms = **80 samples** and there are at most `MAX_AMP` = **160** harmonics `[src: defines.h]`;
   `P_MIN_S` 2.5 ms and `P_MAX_S` 20 ms give the pitch limits (period 20..160 samples).
3. Window the 160-point result with a **triangular** window `t(n) = n/80` for `n` = 0..79 and `2 - n/80` for `n` =
   80..159 `[pdf eq. 13]`; adjacent windows 80 samples apart sum to 1 `[pdf eq. 14]`. The PDF says the shape does not
   matter as long as that sum holds. The source has a synthesis-window overlap constant `TW_S` = 5 ms = **40 samples**
   (a trapezoidal window with 40-sample ramps), and the window buffer is allocated for 2 x 80 = 160 samples; the shape and
   length actually used come from a routine in a file that was not read `[src: defines.h, codec2.c @ 310777b]`. The
   black-box ramps do not look trapezoidal: the amplitude of a steady harmonic moves steadily over 160 samples with a
   change of slope at the sub-frame center `[exp: tools/blackbox/x07_interpolation.py part C]`, which is what two
   80-sample triangles give. The coded window (OQ-6) stays open; use the triangle, which reproduces the measured ramps.
4. Which 160 samples: the block is **centered on index 0 of the IDFT output, wrapping circularly**: its first 80 samples
   (the rising half of the window) come from the last 80 samples of the IDFT output and its last 80 (the falling half)
   from the first 80 `[pdf §3.3, eq. 15]`. The printed index offsets in that equation differ by one sample from this
   reading; that one-sample question is part of OQ-6.
5. Overlap-add: output samples `0..79` of the sub-frame are the first half of the windowed block plus the stored second
   half of the previous sub-frame's block; the second half is stored for the next sub-frame `[pdf §3.3, eq. 15]`.
6. Rounding to int16 with saturation at +-32767 (observed clipping, x08).

**FFT size.** Both transforms the decoder needs are 512 points: the real FFT of the LPC coefficients (`FFT_ENC`) and
the inverse real FFT of the synthesis (`FFT_DEC`) `[src: defines.h, codec2.c @ 310777b]`. One 512-point real FFT and its
inverse therefore serve the envelope and the synthesis; the transform for the minimum-phase filter phase (section 7, OQ-5)
is not known.

## 9. Lost frames `[c2fx design]`

Upstream's decoder has no lost-frame handling (it always decodes the frame it is given). This section is **our own
design**: the library's decode call takes a null frame pointer to mean "frame lost".

- Repeat the previous frame's model (w0, LSPs, voicing as decoded for sub-frame b) for the lost frame, with the
  energy index lowered by a fixed number of steps per consecutive lost frame (starting proposal: 2 steps = 3.125 dB),
  for a fixed maximum run (starting proposal: 4 frames = 80 ms). After the run, output silence and keep the state so a
  good frame next fades in through the normal start-up path.
- Sub-frame a of the lost frame is the interpolation of the previous b with the repeated (decayed) b, as for any frame.
- The run length and the decay are parameters to be fixed with the float model; the requirement is that output stays
  bounded and mutes after the repeat limit. The constants above are starting points, not facts about upstream.

Deciding that a frame is bad stays with the caller (the M17 layer).

## 10. Encoder

The encoder writes the frame of section 2. Two things are specified here, and they are not the same kind of requirement.

**What must match** is the quantizer: given the analysis values for a codec frame, the indices in the frame are
determined (10.1). Another decoder hears those bits. The indices are Gray-coded into the fields of section 2.1.

**What the library may do its own way** is the analysis that produces those values (10.2): a high-pass, the window
and the DFT, the pitch estimate, the voicing decision, the LPC and LSP analysis, and the energy estimate. The
published method and its published constants are stated there. A different method is acceptable. It is held to the
agreement in 10.3, which is measured from `c2enc` bits and nothing else.

Upstream's tools are used as black boxes. `c2sim --rate 3200 --lpc 10 --dump_pitch_e` writes one text row per 10 ms
with four numbers: analysis `w0` in radians per sample, a second number that this spec does not interpret, a voicing
flag (0 or 1), and the analysis energy. `c2sim --rate 3200 --lspEWov` writes the 92-byte record of `vectors/README.md`:
ten unquantized LSP frequencies in radians, then energy and `w0` after the 3200 quantizers. Rows below are numbered
from 0. Codec frame `k` contains 10 ms rows `2k` and `2k+1`.

### 10.1 Quantizers

Checked on every codec frame of every PCM file in `tests/data` (14 files, 2260 frames, including the first frame):
the w0 index, the energy index, the ten LSP indices and both voicing bits matched the rules below on all 2260
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x14_corpus_fields.py]`. The same rules on controlled signals are x11, x12 and x13.

#### 10.1.1 Which 10 ms frame

Analysis runs every 10 ms. The window for row `s` is centered on input sample `(s-1)*80`, so row 0 is centered 80
samples before the first input sample and the samples before the start are zero (10.2.2). Codec frame `k` then
carries `[exp: tools/blackbox/x11_w0_quant.py]`, `[exp: tools/blackbox/x12_energy_quant.py]`,
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x17_voicing_bits.py]`:

| Field | Taken from |
|---|---|
| w0 index, energy index, ten LSP indices | row `2k+1`, the window centered on input sample `160k` |
| voicing bit 0 | the voicing flag of row `2k`, centered on sample `160k-80` |
| voicing bit 1 | the voicing flag of row `2k+1` |

On a glide from 80 to 320 Hz the w0 index matched row `2k+1` on 47 of 47 frames and row `2k` on 13 of 47 (x11).
On loud and quiet blocks 640 samples long, the energy index matched row `2k+1` on 47 of 47 and row `2k` on 28 of 47,
and the two rows disagreed with each other on 19 frames (x12). The PDF's decimation to 20 ms, dropping every second
set of model parameters `[pdf §2.4, §3.7]`, is this choice of row. Voicing is not dropped: both flags are sent.

The window centered on sample `160k` runs from sample `160k-139` to `160k+139`, so it covers the previous frame's
tail and all but the last 20 samples of frame `k`. The decoder plays the parameters of frame `k` at output sample
`160k+160` (section 4), 160 samples after this analysis center.

#### 10.1.2 `w0` index

Let `f0` be the analysis fundamental of row `2k+1`, in Hz (`w0` from the pitch dump times `8000/(2*pi)`). The
index is the nearest point of the grid in section 3.2, clipped to 0..127
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x11_w0_quant.py]`:

```
i = clip( round( (f0 - 50) * 128 / 350 ) , 0, 127 )
```

`round` is round-to-nearest. The closest raw index in the pulse-train sweep came 0.072 of a step from a midpoint
and took the nearer code. An exact midpoint was not produced.

The top of the grid is index 127 at 397.27 Hz (section 3.2). An analysis of exactly 400 Hz has raw index 128 and
is clipped to 127: a 400 Hz pulse train analysed at 400.00 Hz and was encoded as 127 (x11). A raw index below 0
was not produced: the lowest analysis pitch in the corpus is 50.00 Hz. On the silence file the analysis pitch is 50.00 Hz in
frame 0 (index 0) and 59.37 Hz in the other 99 frames (index 3) (x14 prints it).

Worked point: analysis 139.40 Hz, raw index 32.695, encoded index 33. The grid points are 137.50 Hz and 140.23 Hz;
139.40 is nearer 140.23.

#### 10.1.3 Energy index

Let `E` be the analysis energy of row `2k+1` (pitch-dump column 4). What that number is, physically, is in 10.2.6.
The index on a positive `E` is `[exp: tools/blackbox/x12_energy_quant.py]`:

```
e = clip( round( (10*log10(E) + 10) * 32/50 ) , 0, 31 )
```

The step `50/32` is 1.5625 dB, the same step the decoder applies (section 3.3). Index 0 stands for -10 dB, so the
value the index reconstructs is

```
E_q = 10^((-10 + e * 50/32) / 10) = 0.1 * 10^(e * 50/320)
```

which is 0.1 at index 0 and about 6978 at index 31. `E <= 0` encodes as index 0. A pulse train at amplitude 80 had
`E = 0.06` and raw index -1.58, and was encoded as 0, so the low clip is real. Full-scale pulses reached raw index
31.26 to 31.29 and were encoded as 31; a raw index above 31.5 was not produced, so the high clip is the same formula
but was not separately forced. The closest positive raw index in the sweep came 0.076 of a step from a midpoint and
took the nearer code. An exact midpoint was not produced.

The decoder's reading of the same index, and the link between the two scales, is in 6.3.

Worked point: `E = 34.52` is 15.38 dB, raw index 16.24, encoded index 16. Index 16 reconstructs to 15.00 dB and
index 17 to 16.56 dB.

#### 10.1.4 LSP difference indices

The unquantized LSP frequencies of row `2k+1` are the first ten floats of the `lspEWov` record, in radians,
converted by `Hz = rad * 8000 / (2*pi)`. They are already in increasing order. The ten indices are chosen one at
a time against the tables of section 3.4 `[exp: tools/blackbox/x13_lsp_quant.py]`:

Let `f_j` be the unquantized frequency of LSP `j` in Hz, and let `Q_j` be the sum of the table entries chosen for steps
1 to `j-1` (`Q_1 = 0`). The target of step `j` is `t_j = f_j - Q_j`. The index `d_j` is the one whose table entry `D_j[d_j]`
is nearest `t_j` in Hz, that is, `d_j` minimises `|D_j[k] - t_j|` over the 32 entries `k` of table `j`. Then
`Q_(j+1) = Q_j + D_j[d_j]`.

The running sum is the quantized one. Choosing each difference from the unquantized LSPs independently (no running
quantized sum) matched 0 of 37 frames of a 140 Hz pulse train and 21 of 306 frames of `high_voice`. The rule above
matched every frame of those signals, of `low_voice`, of `test_transmission`, of noise, and of an 80 Hz sine.

A target below the first table entry, 25 Hz, selects index 0. That happened 113 times across the 80 Hz sine and
the three voice files, with targets from 0.00 to 24.79 Hz (72 of them under 5 Hz); every one of them was index 0. A
target above the last table entry happened 164 times; the nearest-entry rule matched the written index on every frame
that contained one, so it takes the last entry. The distance is in Hz.
Because the tables are uniform in Hz, nearest in Hz and nearest in radians are the same choice.

Targets within 0.025 Hz of a midpoint between two entries (the two distances differed by less than 0.05 Hz) took
the nearer entry in every such case: 24 cases across the voice files, the noise and the 80 Hz sine (x13 prints the
total). An exact midpoint was not produced.

`[c2fx design]` What the library's own LSP analysis does when it finds fewer than ten roots, or roots out of order, is its
choice; the quantizer rule above is stated for ten ordered frequencies, and 10.3 judges the result.

#### 10.1.5 Voicing bits

Bit 0 is the voicing flag of row `2k`. Bit 1 is the voicing flag of row `2k+1`. Each flag is the 0 or 1 in the
pitch dump, copied through, not combined with the other row
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x17_voicing_bits.py]`, `[exp: tools/blackbox/x14_corpus_fields.py]`. On pulses, noise, a pure
tone, and tone-plus-noise at 6 dB and at 0 dB, the bits mismatched the flags on 0 of 48 frames each. How the flag
itself is decided is 10.2.4, and the library may decide differently inside 10.3. What the decoder does with the
two bits is still OQ-1; this section only says which flags the encoder writes.

### 10.2 Analysis the library may replace

Each item gives the published method `[pdf]`, then what was measured `[exp]`, then the room the library has
`[c2fx design]`.

#### 10.2.1 Input high-pass

The PDF does not specify a high-pass on the encoder input. It mentions high-pass filtering only as a reason the
fundamental can be missing from the signal the pitch estimator sees `[pdf §3.4]`. The pitch estimator's own notch
(10.2.3) is applied after the squarer, not to the input.

An impulse's LPC energy matches the raw samples with no filter in front of the window (10.2.2, ratio 1.00000).
A constant input encodes as energy index 0 on every frame but the first (index 26, where the step from zero is
in the window; x14 prints it for `dc_8000`), which is also what an all-pole model does with a constant, so that fact
does not by itself show a high-pass.

`[c2fx design]` The library may high-pass the input. It does not have to. A high-pass changes the energy and the
LSPs, so it has to stay inside 10.3.

#### 10.2.2 Analysis window

Published `[pdf §3.2]`: overlapping windows of `Nw = 279` samples (odd), advanced by `N = 80` samples (10 ms).
The weight at offset `n` from the start of the 279-point block is a Hann window,

```
w[i] = 1/2 - 1/2 * cos(2*pi*i / 278),   i = 0 .. 278
```

and the block is scaled so that the sum of the squared weights is `1/N_dft`. The PDF states that normalisation
and uses `N_dft = 512` for the pitch estimator (10.2.3). It does not say in words where, in the input, the center
of row 0 sits.

Measured `[exp: tools/blackbox/x15_analysis_window.py]`: row `s` is this 279-point window centered on input sample
`(s-1)*80`, with the sum of squared weights equal to `1/512`. An impulse of amplitude 20000, at several positions,
gives a dumped energy equal to the order-10 LPC gain of that windowed impulse with ratio 1.00000, including the
row whose center is before sample 0. Moving the impulse by one sample moves the two sidelobes the way this window
predicts (1034 and 1160 against a center of 7492).

`[c2fx design]` The library may use another window. The quantizer in 10.1.3 sees the energy this window produced,
so a different window has to stay inside the energy agreement of 10.3.

#### 10.2.3 Pitch estimation

Published `[pdf §3.4]`. The estimate is in the range 50-400 Hz. The estimator takes 320 samples (40 ms). It squares
the input, then applies the notch `(1 - z^-1) / (1 - 0.95 z^-1)` to remove the DC the squarer creates. A 48-tap
FIR low-pass with cutoff 600 Hz follows, then decimation by 5, a window, and a 512-point DFT. The global peak of
that spectrum is not taken blindly: submultiples of its bin (`k/2`, `k/3`, ... down toward 50 Hz) are eligible
when they are local peaks above a threshold, and the threshold is lowered for candidates near the previous frame's
estimate. The coarse estimate is then refined by maximising the sum, over the harmonics, of the squared magnitude
of the analysis spectrum at each harmonic's center bin, over a small range around the coarse value. The PDF says
the post-processing differs from the author's earlier write-up and that the threshold is experimentally derived; it
gives neither the threshold nor the width of the refinement. Those are OQ-11. The PDF also says another pitch estimator may be used if it is
in the same cost and accuracy class.

Measured `[exp: tools/blackbox/x16_pitch_behaviour.py]`, `[exp: tools/blackbox/x11_w0_quant.py]`. Pulse trains from
55 Hz to 385 Hz in steps of 15 Hz (23 rates) were estimated within a ratio 0.991 to 1.006 of the pulse rate (x16 prints
every rate). Pure tones are not estimated at the tone frequency. At amplitude 12000, 10 ms row 17:

| 75 | 136.0 | 31 | 1 |
| 100 | 173.9 | 45 | 1 |
| 125 | 210.5 | 59 | 1 |
| 150 | 244.9 | 71 | 1 |

Every one of those tones was voiced. Several have the pitch dump's second column near 0 (0.00 at 125, 150 and

Above the design range, pulse trains at 420 Hz and at 500 Hz were estimated at 400.0 Hz (then clipped to index

`[c2fx design]` The library may use its own pitch estimator, including its own octave check. It is held to 10.3:
the w0 index within ±2 on voiced frames, and octave errors on at most 2% of voiced frames. Matching this tone
table is not required. The quantizer in 10.1.2 is applied to whatever fundamental the estimator returns.

#### 10.2.4 Voicing decision

Published `[pdf §3.5]`. For each harmonic band up to about 1 kHz, the error is taken between the windowed spectrum
and a single complex sinusoid at that harmonic's center bin, the complex amplitude being the least-squares fit
over the band. An SNR is the harmonic power divided by that error, summed over the bands up to the one nearest
1 kHz. The frame is voiced when that SNR is above 6 dB. The PDF then says several further rules fix common
mistakes, and points at the source for them. Those rules are OQ-12. The two flags, one per 10 ms row, are what
10.1.5 writes.

Measured `[exp: tools/blackbox/x17_voicing_bits.py]`. An 180 Hz sine of amplitude 8000 mixed with gaussian noise,
SNR defined as `10*log10` of the sine's mean square (`8000^2 / 2`) over the noise mean square. Fraction of 10 ms
rows flagged voiced, after the first four rows:

| Time-domain SNR | Voiced rows |
|---|---|
| 20 dB | 95/95 |
| 12 dB | 63/95 |
| 6 dB | 8/95 |
| 3 dB | 2/95 |
| 0 dB | 32/95 |
| -3 dB | 33/95 |
| -6 dB | 25/95 |

The published 6 dB figure is not this time-domain SNR: at 6 dB almost every row was unvoiced, and at 0 dB and
below a third of the rows were voiced again. Pure tones are voiced (10.2.3). The post-rules are doing something
the 6 dB sentence alone does not capture.

`[c2fx design]` The library makes its own voicing decision. It is held to agreeing with upstream's two bits on at
least 90% of bits (10.3). Reproducing the table above is not required.

#### 10.2.5 LPC and the LSP frequencies

Published `[pdf §3.7]`. Every 10 ms, order `p = 10`. The coefficients minimise the squared prediction error
(the usual autocorrelation method and Levinson-Durbin recursion; the PDF names the method only as least squares
and points at a textbook). The gain `G` is the rms of that error, and the frame energy is `E = G^2`. The LPC
polynomial is split into the symmetric and antisymmetric parts `P(z)` and `Q(z)` of section 5, and the LSP
frequencies are the ordered roots of those two polynomials on the unit circle, `0 < f_1 < ... < f_10 < 4000` Hz.

Measured `[exp: tools/blackbox/x15_analysis_window.py]`: the gain of this recursion on the window of 10.2.2
reproduces the dumped energy of an impulse exactly and a pulse-train vowel to a ratio between 0.99994 and 1.00025. Through
the energy quantizer of 10.1.3 it also reproduces the written index on the speech files (`high_voice` 309 of 309 frames,
`low_voice` 306 of 307, `test_transmission` 99 of 104, `ten_second_countdown` 503 of 540; within one index on 100%, 100%,
98% and 97%), on noise, silence, DC and the clipped 200 Hz sine (100 of 100 each). It does not on the unclipped pure
tones and the chirp: the 100, 150, 200, 300 and 400 Hz sine files and `chirp_80_400hz` match on 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 and 7 of
100 frames (within one index: 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 11). On the 200 Hz sine the dumped energy is 0.359 where this gain is 0.0013;
on a 20 Hz sine the dumped energy alternates between about 0.05 and 28, repeating every period, while the unclamped
gain stays under 0.001. Over the whole corpus the gain matches the written index on 1629 of 2260 frames, within one index
on 1658 (73%). That is OQ-10. The quantizer of 10.1.3 was still applied to the dumped number, including on those files
in x14.

`[c2fx design]` The library may use its own LPC and its own root finder. The indices are held to 10.3. On speech this
gain gives the written energy index; on the pure tones and the chirp it does not, and the library does not try to
reproduce upstream there (see the defaults table and the energy bar of 10.3).

#### 10.2.6 The energy number

`E` in 10.1.3 is `G^2` from 10.2.5 for an impulse and for a pulse-train vowel `[pdf §3.7]`,
`[exp: tools/blackbox/x15_analysis_window.py]`. It is not that gain for pure tones and the chirp (OQ-10). The
PDF also describes the energy as the average level of the harmonic amplitudes `[pdf §2.5]`. Those two readings
agree when the residual is the scale of a flat excitation through `1/A(z)`; the number that is quantized is the
one the pitch dump reports, and on impulses, pulse-train vowels, noise and the speech files that number is this `G^2`.

### 10.3 Agreement the library encoder is held to

Compare the library's 3200 frames with `c2enc 3200` on the same int16 PCM, a whole number of 160-sample frames,
frame by frame. Read the fields as in section 2 (Gray-decode every multi-bit field). No internal counter or
struct name is part of the requirement. The bars:

| What | Bar | Counted over |
|---|---|---|
| w0 index | absolute difference at most 2, on at least 90% of the frames | frames on which both encoders set voicing bit 1 |
| voicing | the bits agree, on at least 90% of the bits | both voicing bits of every frame |
| energy index | absolute difference at most 1, on at least 95% of the frames | every frame |
| LSP envelope | mean of the per-frame distance at most 1.5 dB, and at most 2% of frames over 4 dB | every frame |
| octave errors | at most 2% of the frames | the same voiced frames as the w0 bar |

The LSP distance of one frame: dequantize both sets of indices to Hz by section 3.4, build `A(z)` by section 5,
and take the rms, over a uniform grid from 100 Hz to 3800 Hz in steps of 10 Hz, of the difference in
`20*log10|1/A(f)|`. That rms is the frame's distance. The mean is the mean of those rms values. A frame over 4 dB
is one whose rms exceeds 4 dB.

An octave error: on a frame counted in the w0 bar, convert both indices to Hz by section 3.2. The frame is an
octave error when the ratio of the larger frequency to the smaller is greater than `sqrt(2)` (the point halfway,
on a log scale, between a ratio of 1 and a ratio of 2).

Each bar is counted **per file** over the PCM files in `tests/data`, and the figures pooled over all of them are
reported as well. The energy bar does not count the six files on which upstream's energy is not the LPC gain of 10.2.5:
`sine_100hz`, `sine_150hz`, `sine_200hz`, `sine_300hz`, `sine_400hz` and `chirp_80_400hz` (OQ-10); the clipped sine
`sine_200hz_clipped` is counted.

`[c2fx design]` These bars are the acceptance test for the library encoder, with this difference: on the four voice files the w0 (at most 2), octave and LSP (over 4 dB) figures are reported, not asserted. What is asserted is voicing and energy agreement, and fidelity to the input, `LSD(decode(ours), input) <= LSD(decode(reference bits), input) + 1 dB` per voice file, through the float decoder. These bars are provisional until the owner's blind listening test of the fixed-point encoder. They are not a claim about how close
upstream is to itself. The synthesized files in `tests/data` are the inputs an implementation is checked on; a
recording of a person is not one of them.

## Defaults for the open items `[c2fx design]`

Where the evidence stops, c2fx uses these, each a design choice rather than a measured fact (except where `[src]`):

| Item | Default |
|---|---|
| Voicing of each sub-frame | Its own bit: bit 0 for sub-frame a, bit 1 for sub-frame b `[src: codec2.c @ 310777b]`. The black-box observation that a steady (1,0) frame decodes like (0,0) is unexplained and c2fx does not reproduce it |
| An unvoiced sub-frame's `w0` and `L` | It keeps its frame's dequantized `w0` (the current frame's, for sub-frame a too) and `L = floor(pi / w0)` |
| `w0` when a voicing change is involved (voiced to unvoiced, or unvoiced to voiced) | The current frame's `w0`; the midpoint of section 4 only when the previous frame's sub-frame b and sub-frame a are both voiced |
| Phase | As section 7: minimum phase of the envelope sampled at the harmonics, a pulse-train excitation phase for voiced harmonics, uniform random phase for unvoiced ones, and for harmonics under the background level; the minimum-phase method (for example a cepstral one) and its transform size, the background-level estimate and its threshold, and a noise generator seeded per decoder state are designed in the implementation phase; none of them is a measured fact |
| Post-filter exponents and the level offset | gamma 0.6, beta 0.275, offset -3.62 dB (the x09 refined best), or the PDF pair with -4.09 dB; always together (6.2) |
| Synthesis window | Two 80-sample triangles, hop 80 (section 8) |
| Initial state | Section 4, start-up |
| Floor in the envelope (6.2) | `[c2fx design]` `eps` = 1e-6 added to the squared magnitude of `A(z)` wherever it is inverted: in `P(k)` and in the denominator of the post-filter ratio; the squared magnitude of `A(z/gamma)` in the numerator is left as it is |
| Output overload (OQ-13) | `[c2fx design]` Saturate at the 16-bit limits. `c2dec` instead lowers the output as the energy index rises once the level is very high; files that reach that level (`sine_200hz_clipped`) will differ from `c2dec` by about 3 dB |
| Energy of a pure tone or a sweep (OQ-10) | `[c2fx design]` The encoder uses the LPC-gain energy of 10.2.5 as specified. On pure tones and sweeps it may sit off upstream's energy index; the energy bar of 10.3 does not count those files, which are named there |
| Pitch search thresholds (OQ-11) | `[c2fx design]` The float model's pitch tracker takes the peak of the published preprocessed spectrum, then keeps a submultiple of it when the bin is a local peak holding at least `C2M_PITCH_SUBHARM_RATIO` (0.55) of the peak, lowered to `C2M_PITCH_NEAR_RATIO` (0.39) within `C2M_PITCH_TRACK_TOL` (20%) of the previous voiced row's pitch and raised to `C2M_PITCH_FAR_RATIO` (0.79) where it would cut that pitch by more than `C2M_PITCH_TRACK_JUMP` (1.6); a submultiple must also hold up in the time domain (`C2M_PITCH_CORR_MARGIN`), below `C2M_PITCH_LOW_HZ` it needs up to `C2M_PITCH_LOW_RATIO`, and a strong non-integer lower peak (`C2M_PITCH_ANY_RATIO`) can win. The result is refined by a harmonic sum (`C2M_PITCH_HS_STEPS` steps of `C2M_PITCH_HS_STEP`). This is a design choice, not part of the bit-exact format: any encoder whose bits decode correctly is conforming. Judged by the w0 and octave bars of 10.3 |
| Voicing post-rules (OQ-12) | `[c2fx design]` The float model applies three rules on top of the published 6 dB band-SNR test (`C2M_VOICING_SNR_DB`), using the first normalized autocorrelation coefficient (the tilt) of the windowed row: tilt below `C2M_VOICING_TILT_REJECT` (0.0) makes a row that passed the SNR test unvoiced; tilt above `C2M_VOICING_TILT_ACCEPT` (0.6) makes a row voiced whatever its SNR; an unvoiced row between two voiced rows becomes voiced when its tilt is above `C2M_VOICING_TILT_BRIDGE` (0.5), which needs 80 samples of look-ahead. The white-noise floor of the LPC analysis (`C2M_LPC_WHITE_NOISE`, 2e-7) is likewise a design choice. None of this is part of the bit-exact format: any encoder whose bits decode correctly is conforming. Judged by the voicing bar of 10.3 |

## What the PDF gives, and which files hold the tables

**What `codec2.pdf` gives for 3200.** The bit allocation (7 pitch, 5 energy, 2 voicing, 50 for the spectral amplitudes);
that LSP **differences** are quantised, at some cost in robustness to bit errors; 20 ms frames with a 10 ms internal
rate and linear interpolation between; the pitch range (50-400 Hz) and `L = floor(pi/w0)`; the structure of the post
filter with `beta = 0.2`, `gamma = 0.5` and a +3 dB low-frequency boost; the zero-order phase model; and the IDFT
overlap-add synthesis with a triangular window and hop 80. It does **not** give the field order, the Gray coding, the
`w0` and energy ranges and steps, the tables, the interpolation weights, the voicing rule, or any constant of the phase
model and the level normalisation; those come from experiment here. For the encoder it gives the 279-point analysis
window, the pitch estimator's structure and the 6 dB voicing test (section 10.2). It does not give the forward
quantizer rules of section 10.1; those are from the black-box runs.

**Which codebook files.** `dlsp1.txt` through `dlsp10.txt` in upstream's `src/codebook/` (one file per LSP difference),
each a header line `1 32` and 32 values in Hz: 10 files, 320 numbers. The other LSP files (`lsp1..10.txt`,
`lsp8910.txt`, `lspjmv*`, `lspvqexp*`) belong to other modes, and `gecb.txt`, `newamp*`, `codes_450.txt` and `train_*`
are not 3200. `w0` and the energy have no table: they are formulas (3.2, 3.3). All ten files follow the two regular
patterns of 3.4.

**Model dumps.** `c2sim ... --modelout <file>` writes a per-10 ms model dump without any source change (record layout in
`tools/blackbox/README.md`); with `--rate 3200` the dumped `w0` is already on the 3200 grid. The record's fields were
inferred from size and values only.
